# DKeramik — business plans

For a ceramist with about two years of study, an own studio, one fair, and a live shop. Companion file: [marketing.md](./marketing.md).

Figures are **planning estimates in euros** for a Lithuania-based solo studio in 2026 (individual activity, no VAT). They are not forecasts. **Conservative** means the offer is worked unevenly. **Target** means the offer is clear and dates or stock actually convert.

A focused year on the 90-day stack is closer to **€12–18k conservative** or **€22–28k target** extra income than to the sum of every column below. That is not a full salary yet. Replacing a job is a year-2–3 problem if workshops become weekly.

Shop operations (orders, stock, invoices): [shop-management.md](./shop-management.md).

---

## Recommendation: a stack, not six businesses

For the next 90 days:

1. **Studio workshops** — cash engine
2. **Three-form tableware capsule** (mug, bowl, small vase × two glazes) in the shop — product
3. **One autumn design-leaning fair + one Christmas date** — amplifier

Park cafe commissions, corporate boxes, and wholesale until the capsule is in the kiln and on camera.

You already have what most beginners do not: a studio, a brand voice (homes, daily rituals, handmade warmth), and a working web shop with invoices.

**This week, in order**

1. Freeze the hero mug and the 2-hour workshop offer (price, what they make, deposit).
2. Put four dates on a graphic.
3. Photograph eight real pieces and list them in the shop.
4. Ask five people you already know to book or to buy a voucher.
5. Apply to one design-leaning autumn date and one Christmas date.

Realistic 90-day cash if the stack runs: **€1,500–3,500**. Empty workshop dates are the main risk, not price.

## Where the money actually is

Handmade one-offs feel like the job. At this stage they are the slowest way to pay rent. Teaching sells time in 2-hour blocks. A repeatable mug can sell while you are not in the room. Fairs concentrate gift buyers. Commissions and shops come later because they need proof you can repeat a glaze.

### Comparison

| Option | Role now | 90-day cash | Year 1 conservative | Year 1 target | Cash to start |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Studio workshops | Do this first | €1,400–2,200 if 8 sessions fill at ~4 people | €4,500 | €10,000 | €80–150 |
| Signature tableware + shop | Build in parallel | €300–800 from the shop | €3,500 | €8,000 | €100–250 |
| Curated fairs and pop-ups | Seasonal accelerator | €400–1,800 across two events | €2,500 | €5,500 | €120–300 |
| Cafes, interiors, private sets | Park until you have proof | €0 unless a warm lead exists | €1,200 | €4,000 | €40 |

| Boutiques and consignment | Visibility with thin margin | €0–400 | €1,000 | €3,000 | €0–30 |

Year-1 columns assume that path is worked as a real offer, not as a hobby beside a full-time job. **Do not add every target together.**

## 1. Studio workshops

**Verdict:** Do this first.  
**When:** Now — this is the cash engine.

### Customer

Adults 25–45 in Kaunas and nearby towns who want a tactile evening: birthdays, dates, friend groups, and people who have never touched clay. Lithuanian-first. Secondary: small teams (4–8) for offsites.

### Offer

Three named offers, not a vague “come play with clay”:

1. **Pirmas molis** — 2 hours, 4–6 people, one piece, firing included, pickup in 3 weeks.
2. **Dviese prie molio** — private couple session.
3. From month 3: a **4-week Thursday course** so the same people return.

### Pricing

- Group **€45–49** per person (market in Kaunas is often €20–30; you can sit slightly above if the studio feels calm and the photos are strong, but do not start at €70).
- Couples **€99** for two.
- Four-week course **€155–175**.
- **50% deposit** to book.
- No cash-on-the-day if you want to avoid a cash register (*kasos aparatas*).

### Unit economics

A 5-person group at €47 is **€235** revenue. Clay, glaze, firing, and tea ≈ €8–12 per person. Net before your time ≈ **€175–195**. Session block is ~3 hours including setup. That is roughly **€60/hour** — far better than a €36 mug that took a week of drying and two firings.

### Capacity

Start with **2 public sessions per month** plus 1 private. Studio + kiln, not Instagram, is the limit: drying racks and bisque/glaze calendar must be batched. Cap groups at 6 until you can teach without ruining pieces. Do not teach more than 3 sessions in a week if you also need to produce for fairs.

### 90-day cash

**€1,400–2,200** if 8 sessions fill at ~4 people.

### Cash to start

**€80–150** for extra tools, aprons, extra clay, printed vouchers.

### First actions

1. Write a one-screen offer: date, time, price, what they make, what to wear, how they pay.
2. Open 4 dates in the next 8 weeks (two weeknights, two Saturdays).
3. Take deposits via the existing shop or a simple bank transfer with your invoice number.
4. Photograph the table as students will see it: stools, clay, natural light — not only finished pots.
5. Decide pickup logistics (Saturday 12–14h window) so you are not doing 20 individual handovers.

### Risks

- No-shows and last-minute cancellations.
- Teaching eats the hours you need to make stock for Christmas.
- Kiln bottleneck if everyone wants large pieces.
- Underpricing to “get started” and then being unable to raise the price.

### 8-week calendar

| When | Work |
| --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Offer text, 4 dates, deposit method, Google Business, 15-photo phone shoot. |
| Week 2 | Publish date 1, partner poster, 3 reels, Facebook Event. |
| Week 3 | Fill leftover seats via Stories and two local Facebook groups (one post, not spam). |
| Week 4 | Run session 1, collect photos, sell 2 vouchers. |
| Weeks 5–6 | Session 2, announce course waitlist. |
| Weeks 7–8 | Sessions 3–4, Christmas voucher graphic, ask 5 students for a one-line review. |

Marketing for this path: [marketing.md — Studio workshops](./marketing.md#1-studio-workshops).

## 2. Signature tableware + shop

**Verdict:** Build in parallel.  
**When:** Now, but as a small capsule — not 40 one-offs.

### Customer

Gift buyers and people furnishing a first real home. They already like your brand line: daily rituals, warmth, handmade variation. They will pay for a mug that looks like the website, not for an unexplained sculptural experiment.

### Offer

Lock **three forms and two glazes**: a morning mug, a bowl, a small vase. That is six SKUs. Optional: a two-mug gift set. Unique gallery pieces stay in the portfolio with for-sale off until someone asks.

The shop already exists. The gap is real photos, stock, and a clear gift story — not more website features.

### Pricing

- Mug **€34–42**
- Bowl **€44–55**
- Small vase **€58–72**
- Two-mug set **€68–78**

Price to a studio wage of at least **€15–18/hour** after materials, or you are donating kiln time. Match Vilnius/Kaunas handmade, not IKEA and not gallery-only prices.

### Unit economics

Materials + glaze + firing for a mug ≈ **€5–9**. If a batch of 8 mugs takes one focused making day plus shared firing, cash margin is good. One-offs destroy margin. Shipping inside Lithuania is already in the shop; default to pickup to keep early orders simple.

### Capacity

If you also teach: **16–32 finished pieces per month** is a serious output. Batch: throw one day, trim one day, glaze one day. Do not switch clay bodies every week.

### 90-day cash

**€300–800** from the shop if photos and stock go live. Most of year-one product cash still arrives at fairs and Christmas.

### Cash to start

**€100–250** for boxes, tissue, stamps, extra clay, one proper photo day.

### First actions

1. Pick the hero mug. Make 12 of it in two glazes before inventing a new shape.
2. Replace placeholder images with 8 real photos (hero, handle, lip, in use with coffee, scale in hands).
3. Put 6–10 pieces on sale in admin with honest stock counts.
4. Write one gift caption in Lithuanian and English. Same voice as the About page.
5. Standardize packing so a mug survives LP Express.

### Risks

- Endless new shapes, empty shop.
- Photographing on a phone against a messy studio wall.
- Selling unique pieces online that you then sell at a fair.
- Waiting for a “collection launch” instead of listing what exists.

### 8-week calendar

| When | Work |
| --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Freeze 3 forms, throw the first dozen. |
| Week 2 | Photo day, update product pages, set prices in admin. |
| Week 3 | Announce “now in the shop” with 3 Stories/day for 4 days. |
| Week 4 | First pickup/ship, ask for a kitchen photo. |
| Weeks 5–8 | Second batch, two-mug set, start a simple waitlist for restocks. |

Marketing for this path: [marketing.md — Signature tableware](./marketing.md#2-signature-tableware--shop).

## 3. Curated fairs and pop-ups

**Verdict:** Seasonal accelerator.  
**When:** Autumn and Christmas first; 4–6 events per year, not every weekend.

### Customer

People who need to touch the work. Christmas gift buyers, interior-curious visitors at design-leaning markets (for example boutique pop-ups at places like Drobės fabrikas), not bargain-bin arena crowds unless you are testing low price points.

### Offer

A stall that looks like a tiny DKeramik kitchen: one styled table setting, mugs at eye level, a small-decor tray under €25, a hero piece, workshop vouchers, and a QR to the shop. Three price bands so people can buy without a speech.

### Pricing

- Small object **€16–25**
- Mug **€34–42**
- Statement **€70–120**
- Vouchers at full workshop price
- Do not discount on the last hour unless the alternative is carrying it home

### Unit economics

Table + travel often **€50–150**. Rule: do not repeat a fair unless **net sales ≥ 3× the stall fee**. A good Christmas Saturday can beat a month of Instagram. A dead fair is a studio day you cannot get back.

### Capacity

Need **40–80 pieces** on the table plus wrapping. That is why the capsule line matters. One person can run a small stall; two is better for Christmas.

### 90-day cash

**€400–1,800** across one design pop-up and one Christmas date if stock and display are ready.

### Cash to start

**€120–300** for stall cloth, stands, bags, lighting, first stall fee.

### First actions

1. List 5 fairs that match interiors/aesthetics, not 20 random *mugių*.
2. Apply early to the best 2 (autumn pop-up + Christmas).
3. Build a packing checklist and a 90-minute setup plan.
4. Print 50 cards with Instagram, shop URL, and workshop dates.
5. Count every piece in/out so shop stock stays true.

### Risks

- Wrong crowd, cheap comparisons, weather, leftover inventory.
- Selling unique pieces that were also online.
- Standing for two days with no email capture — visibility dies on Sunday night.

### 8-week calendar

| When | Work |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | Apply and pay deposits. |
| Weeks 3–5 | Produce specifically for the stall (the capsule, not experiments). |
| Week 6 | Display rehearsal at home, photograph it. |
| Week 7 | Announce. |
| Week 8 | Event, then list leftovers the same night. |

Marketing for this path: [marketing.md — Fairs](./marketing.md#3-curated-fairs-and-pop-ups).

## 4. Cafes, interiors, private sets

**Verdict:** Park until you have proof.  
**When:** After 20+ finished pieces in use and a short photo set. Not the next 90 days as a main bet.

### Customer

A cafe that wants 24 mugs that match the room; a couple that wants a wedding dinner set; an interior designer who needs 8 objects for a shoot or a home.

### Offer

A one-page spec: clay, glaze, size, lead time (8–12 weeks), minimums, **40% to start**. Cafe mug runs and 6–12 piece private dinner sets only. No “make me something, I’ll know it when I see it.”

### Pricing

- Cafe mug wholesale **€22–28** at 24+ pieces
- Private dinner set **€450–900**
- Rush fees are real — kiln schedules are not elastic

### Unit economics

High ticket, slow cash. One cafe job can equal two months of workshops, or it can stall Christmas stock. Quote materials + kiln + hours × **€20–25**, then add 20% for breakage and meetings.

### Capacity

**One commission at a time** in year one. Never during November–December production.

### 90-day cash

**€0** unless a warm lead already exists. Treat any inquiry as upside.

### Cash to start

**€40** for a printed line sheet and 3 sample mugs you can leave for a week.

### First actions

1. Photograph 6 pieces in real use (your kitchen is enough).
2. Write lead time and minimums so you do not negotiate from zero.
3. Pick 8 cafes whose interiors already match your glaze, not 40 cold emails.
4. Walk in with samples between 14:00–16:00, ask for the owner, leave the sheet.

### Risks

- Unpaid design time, scope creep, glaze that does not match the moodboard, payment after delivery.
- Becoming an unpaid interior accessory for a venue that will not credit you.

### Calendar

Do not schedule this in the first 8 weeks. When the capsule exists: one week for the line sheet, one week of 4 visits, then stop and return to production.

Marketing for this path: [marketing.md — Commissions](./marketing.md#4-cafes-interiors-private-sets).

## 5. Corporate and wedding gifts

**Verdict:** October–November play.  
**When:** Build the box in September. Sell in October. Deliver by early December. Second season: weddings in May–June.

### Customer

HR/office managers buying 8–30 gifts; wedding couples who want a useful favor, not plastic. Companies that already buy “nice Lithuanian” food boxes are the competitors — you win on an object they keep.

### Offer

One SKU: gift box = 1 signature mug + care card + optional handwritten tag. **Minimum 8.** Logo stamp or wrap as a paid extra. No 12-option catalogs.

### Pricing

- Box **€42–55**
- Logo extra **€6–10**
- Company invoice via the shop you already have
- **50%** to start production

### Unit economics

A 12-box order at €48 is **€576**. Time is packing and communication as much as throwing. Profitable if the mug is the same capsule mug, not a new design per logo.

### Capacity

Cap at **40 boxes** in year one. After that you are a factory without staff.

### 90-day cash

**€0–600** unless you already have a company contact. Real window is Q4.

### Cash to start

**€50** for boxes and a one-page PDF.

### First actions

1. Photograph one packed box.
2. Write a 1-page PDF: price, deadline (order by 10 Nov), invoice, pickup/shipping.
3. Send to 15 people who work in offices (accountants, agencies, clinics) — warm network first.
4. Put “Kalėdinės dovanos” on Instagram from 1 October, not 15 December.

### Risks

- Late orders you cannot fire in time.
- Custom logos that crack in the kiln.
- Chasing 2-box orders.

### Calendar

| When | Work |
| --- | --- |
| September | Box design, PDF, 8 extra mugs as buffer. |
| October | Outreach weeks. |
| November | Produce and invoice. |

Do not invent this in the first month of studio season.

## 6. Boutiques and consignment

**Verdict:** Visibility with thin margin.  
**When:** After the capsule is repeatable. 3 shops beat 15.

### Customer

Interior shops, florists, concept stores, museum shops. Buyers who already sell €30–80 objects. They need restock, not a one-time artist dump.

### Offer

Start consignment **50/50** if they are the right room; move to wholesale at ~50% of retail, **prepaid**, once something sells twice. Line sheet: 6 SKUs, retail and wholesale, restock time 4–6 weeks.

### Pricing

Retail as in the shop. Wholesale ≈ half. Do not undercut your own website price.

### Unit economics

A mug that retails at €38 yields ~**€19** wholesale. Fine for reach, bad as the only channel. Cap how much stock sits in someone else’s cabinet unpaid.

### Capacity

3 locations × 8 pieces = **24 pieces off-site**. That is a month of making. Track it like the admin inventory.

### 90-day cash

**€0–400**. Shops pay slowly on consignment.

### Cash to start

**€0** extra if stock exists; **€30** printed line sheets.

### First actions

1. Walk into 5 shops with 4 pieces in a box. Ask who buys. Get a name.
2. Leave a line sheet, not a speech.
3. Agree in writing: prices, commission, breakage, Instagram credit, how you get unsold work back.
4. Visit once a month. Ghosted consignment is lost inventory.

### Risks

Unpaid breakage, dusty corners, shops that never post, brand looking cheaper than the studio.

### Calendar

After 20 finished capsule pieces: one week of visits, then production to refill the studio first.
